# OpenQP Community and Sustainable Development Statement

OpenQP exists to make advanced quantum-chemistry methods inspectable,
reproducible, and practically usable while sustaining the engineering required
to turn research software into dependable scientific infrastructure.

## A shared commitment

The OpenQP licensing model is intended to balance three legitimate interests:

1. **Researchers and educators** should be able to study the implementation,
   reproduce published results, modify the source for qualifying internal
   academic work, teach with it, and publish the scientific results they obtain
   without a license fee.
2. **Commercial users** should have a clear path to evaluate, integrate, and use
   OpenQP under written terms appropriate for commercial R&D, products, and
   services.
3. **The OpenQP team and company** need durable funding for scientific
   validation, software engineering, documentation, platform support, security,
   and the continued development of new methods.

No single side can sustain the project alone. Academic use broadens scientific
testing, education, scrutiny, and impact. Commercial licensing allows
organizations that receive business value from OpenQP to help finance the work
that keeps the platform accurate, maintained, and available to the research
community.

## Why this model

A fully closed program would make independent scientific inspection and
reproducibility harder. A permissive license for the complete platform would,
on the other hand, allow commercial value to be extracted without supporting
the expensive validation and maintenance on which all users depend.

OpenQP therefore uses a source-available research/commercial model for current
releases: qualifying academic and nonprofit research is available without a
license fee, while use by or for a commercial entity requires a separate
commercial license. This is a deliberate sustainability model, not a claim that
the current Research License is an OSI-approved open-source license.

The detailed legal permissions and restrictions are stated in `LICENSE` and
`LICENSING.md`. If this statement and those legal terms differ, the legal terms
control.

## Scientific context

This approach is informed by the computational-chemistry community's broader
discussion of sustainable software. Di Felice *et al.* identify sustainability
and interoperability as pressing scientific-infrastructure problems, emphasize
stable public interfaces, modular design, validation, documentation, and
professional stewardship, and note that sustainable funding may include
software monetization. They also make clear that sustainable scientific
software may be open or closed source, while reproducibility and long-term
accessibility still have to be addressed deliberately.

OpenQP adopts those principles through inspectable research source, documented
and replaceable module boundaries, reproducible validation, and a commercial
license that can fund continued stewardship without charging qualifying
academic users a license fee.

Reference: R. Di Felice *et al.*, “A Perspective on Sustainable Computational
Chemistry Software Development and Integration,” *J. Chem. Theory Comput.*
**2023**, *19*, 7056–7076.
[https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.3c00419](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.3c00419)

## What we aim to keep open to scientific scrutiny

Subject to privacy, security, third-party rights, and contractual obligations,
the project aims to publish and maintain:

- scientific papers and method descriptions;
- validation and regression results;
- documented input and output formats and stable interoperability interfaces;
- examples and reproducible research workflows; and
- transparent version, provenance, and third-party license records.

Third-party libraries and data always remain under their own licenses. OpenQP's
license does not take away rights granted directly by those third parties.

## Contributions and stewardship

Scientific discussion, issue reports, benchmark data, reproducibility findings,
and interoperability proposals are welcome. Code contributions require prior
approval and a written contributor agreement before they can be merged.

The intended contributor policy is researcher-friendly: contributors should
retain the right to use their original work, while granting Open Quantum Inc.
the durable copyright and patent permissions needed to maintain, distribute,
and license the combined platform for both research and commercial users. The
project will not treat a pull request or a notice in a repository as a silent
transfer of ownership.

This clear chain of authority protects contributors, academic users, customers,
and the project itself. It also allows commercial licensing revenue to support
one coherent, scientifically reviewed code base rather than forcing the
community and company into incompatible forks.

## Historical releases

OpenQP v1.2.1 and earlier tagged releases remain available under the GPLv3 terms
that accompanied those copies. OpenQP v1.3.0 is the first planned tagged release
under the current research/commercial model. Historical rights are respected;
new development is licensed prospectively under the terms shipped with each
version.

## Our measure of success

The model succeeds only if it produces both strong science and sustained
software. We will judge it by whether researchers can scrutinize and reproduce
the methods, whether commercial users receive reliable value, and whether the
resulting support measurably improves OpenQP for the whole scientific
community.
